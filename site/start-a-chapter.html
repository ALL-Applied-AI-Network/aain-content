The playbook, in one screen

Your first three weeks decide your year.

Miss that window and you spend the rest of the year recruiting instead of building. Here is the timeline from the chapter that grew from 20 members to 500.

Week 0

Org fair.

Collect an interest list. Lock the day, time and room before you recruit, and never move them. One moved meeting costs about 15% of your attendance, for good.

Week 1

Say the whole year out loud.

The pitch, in order: other chapters in the network, speakers who come to you, it has landed people jobs, it takes work, a real project with upperclassmen on your team.

Week 3

First speaker event.

Book a great speaker. Same slot every other week from here on, free food every time. That is the best money you will spend.

Week 5

Project kick-off.

Teams of five to eight with an upperclassman mentor, announced in the room. Pick one hackathon or one Innovation Lab this semester, not both.

Full detail, with what went wrong the first time: the Getting Started playbook. Speakers, projects, sponsors and hackathons each have their own.

Get sponsored

How sponsors find you.

Sponsors sign up at the portal and browse chapters by members, recent events and tech focus, then send a proposal with a tier and an amount. You accept or decline from your dashboard. An Innovation Lab is $5,000, direct chapter sponsorships start lower, and every dollar lands in your chapter’s account.

We take no cut. Sponsorships are tax-deductible gifts to a 501(c)(3), restricted to your chapter. The Foundation holds the money; your chapter directs the spend.
Ask for a speaker slot first, not money. Most of our sponsors were speakers first. The Sponsorship playbook has the sequence and the first-meeting script.
