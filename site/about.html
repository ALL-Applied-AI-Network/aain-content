ALL Applied AI Network Foundation

Students who build real AI before they graduate.

ALL Applied AI Network Foundation is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit that gives university students the playbooks, software and fiscal sponsorship to start and run applied AI clubs.

What we do What’s different Who it’s for Team How it works Key facts FAQ

What ALL does

Everything a student club needs to run a serious applied AI program, free.

  • Playbooks and films

    Six playbooks cover the first five weeks, speaker events, sponsorship, Innovation Labs, hackathons and research groups, with three short films. Each one is what the MSOE AI Club learned by running it, including what went wrong.

  • A chapter dashboard

    Officers run the roster, events, check-in and attendance from one place. Members check in with a QR code and earn points and badges on their profile.

  • A chapter website

    Each chapter can have a live site on all-ai-network.org that updates from the dashboard, so officers don’t maintain a second website.

  • Sponsorship and the money

    Companies sponsor a chapter, fund a prize pool or back an Innovation Lab through the sponsor portal. The Foundation holds the funds and issues a virtual card per event, so no student treasurer needs a bank account.

  • A way to get hired

    Sponsors the Foundation approves can see member profiles and recruit from the room. Any member with an account can turn that off with one switch.

  • A learning tree

    A free map of the best AI learning resources that already exist, sorted into a skill tree a student can follow from first steps to real projects.

What makes ALL different

  1. 01

    Free, and we take no cut

    Chapters pay no dues and no fees, and the Foundation takes $0 from sponsorships and gifts, so everything but card processing reaches the chapter the giver chose. Fiscal sponsors commonly keep a percentage of each gift; we keep none.

  2. 02

    Built by a chapter that did it

    The playbooks come from the MSOE AI Club, which grew from 20 active members to 500+ at a school that graduates fewer than 100 computer science students a year. Everything in them was run there first.

  3. 03

    Real problems from real companies

    In an Innovation Lab a company brings a real problem, funds a prize pool and mentors a student team for eight weeks. Direct Supply’s Applied AI Director: “We’ve hired more quality candidates through Innovation Labs than any career fair we’ve attended.”

  4. 04

    We hold the money

    Chapters raise money through the Foundation, which holds it for them, so a student club can accept a sponsorship without opening its own bank account. Online gifts get an emailed tax receipt.

  5. 05

    Open source

    The playbooks are licensed CC BY-SA 4.0, and the code for this site and the chapter website template is MIT-licensed, on GitHub. Any school can read, copy or improve them without signing up.

Who uses ALL

  • University students who want to start an AI club at a school that doesn’t have one.
  • Officers of an existing AI, machine learning or data science club who want the playbooks and the software, without giving up their club’s name.
  • Faculty advisors who want a club that keeps running after its founding officers graduate.
  • Students of any major and any experience level: chapter events are free, and nobody needs experience or a sign-up to attend.
  • Companies that want to sponsor a chapter, fund a prize pool, put engineers in front of students or recruit from project teams.
  • Donors who want a tax-deductible gift to reach one specific chapter.

The team behind ALL

How it started

This began as the AI Club at the Milwaukee School of Engineering, founded by Xander Neuwirth in 2020. The first year ran on a budget of zero and mostly went wrong: weekly technical workshops that were too advanced for freshmen and too basic for juniors, and not many people came back.

Two things worked: speaker events anyone could walk into, and projects scoped with a company that ran a couple of months with older students mentoring younger ones. That version of the club grew past 500 members, and other schools started asking how to copy it. We set the first few up by hand, realized that didn’t scale, and turned it into a nonprofit.

How ALL works

  1. Onboard in about thirty minutes. Set the chapter up yourself, with no call and no dues. You leave with a roster, a live chapter site, your first event published and a sponsor invited.
  2. Or claim the club you already run. An existing club keeps its name and its site and adds the dashboard, the sponsor portal and the playbooks.
  3. Run the year from the dashboard. Officers publish events, members check in with a QR code, and each check-in earns points and badges on the member’s profile.
  4. Bring in sponsors. Invite a company from the dashboard. It works through the sponsor portal, and its money comes through the Foundation.

Questions go to hello@all-ai-network.org. The Getting Started playbook covers a chapter’s first five weeks.

How the money works

We’re a 501(c)(3) in Milwaukee, Wisconsin, and we hold the money, so a student treasurer never has to become an accountant.

ALL Applied AI Network Foundation is a nonprofit corporation recognized as tax-exempt under section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code. Chapters are projects of the Foundation rather than separate legal entities.

When someone gives to a chapter, the gift goes to the Foundation, restricted to that chapter’s charitable work. The Foundation holds and accounts for the funds; the chapter spends against them. We do not take a platform fee.

Frequently asked questions

Does it cost anything to start a chapter?

No. There are no dues and no fees, and the Foundation takes $0 from the money a chapter raises; only card processing fees come out of card payments.

Do members need AI experience, or to be computer science majors?

No. Chapter events are free and open to anyone, with no sign-up needed to attend. The founding chapter grew through software and computer engineering, UX, business and mechanical engineering students as much as through computer science.

Our school already has an AI club. Can it join?

Yes. An existing club claims its chapter instead of starting a new one, keeps its name and its site, and adds the dashboard, the sponsor portal and the playbooks.

How does sponsorship money reach a chapter?

A company or donor gives to the Foundation, restricted to that chapter’s work, and gets a receipt stating what is tax-deductible. The Foundation holds and accounts for the money, and the chapter spends it, usually through a virtual card for each event.

Is a chapter its own legal entity?

No. Chapters are projects of the Foundation, so a student club doesn’t need its own bank account to accept a sponsorship. Some universities route corporate sponsorships through their own advancement office, and a chapter follows its school’s rules.

Do sponsors see student profiles?

Sponsors the Foundation has approved can browse member profiles, and each member also has a public profile page; both are on by default. Any member with an account can turn both off with one switch under Profile → Share, and the privacy policy has the details.

What is ALL’s relationship with NVIDIA?

The Foundation has an exploratory partnership with NVIDIA to potentially scale the MSOE AI Club’s model beyond MSOE. NVIDIA engineers have spoken at the founding chapter, which is a pilot member of the NVIDIA Student Network.
