# Innovation Labs

A company brings a real problem, mixed teams build for eight weeks, and the company leaves with a working prototype. The easiest way into hands-on work.

Innovation Labs are 8-week industry-sponsored competitions, kicked off at the week 5 project kick-off. The founding chapter has run them with Brady Corporation (Fall 2024), Juno (Spring 2025), Persistent Systems with Databricks, Xorbix (Fall 2025), and Rockwell Automation with NVIDIA (Fall 2026, with UW-Milwaukee). Sell it to students as a project with a company's name on it, teammates a few years ahead of them, and a portfolio piece before Christmas break. No experience needed.

## Start here

1. Lead with the problem their team actually has, and put NDA and IP on the table in the first meeting. Students don't sign NDAs.
2. The shape: about eight weeks from the week 5 kick-off, teams of six to nine mixed across years, and a sponsor engineer holding weekly office hours.
3. Close the loop: cleaned-up code to the sponsor within two weeks, a letter for every finisher, and an email 30 days after the showcase about the next one.

## Pitch a sponsor

**Lead with the problem their team actually has, not the charity. Then settle IP before students see the brief.**

1. **First meeting (30–45 min).** Learn what they want from students: hires, visibility, a problem solved. Offer pathways, not a price: a talk, a Lab, a research problem. Have the NDA conversation in meeting 1, every time.
2. **Second meeting (30 min).** Agree the pathway and the budget. $5K is our default prize pool for a Lab.
3. **Third meeting (45 min).** Write the brief together: the data, the metric, and what a win looks like.
4. **Agreement, then kickoff.** Promotion starts four weeks out.

**Our NDA stance:** we don't ask students to sign NDAs, because a required signature lowers participation. Agree up front what data the sponsor shares, who owns what, and whether code can be public after the Lab. The first Lab lost two weeks in legal back-and-forth by leaving this until September.

**Ask for a mentor.** A sponsor engineer with weekly office hours is what makes it a Lab and not a long hackathon.

**In the dashboard:** **Sponsors → Requests → A Problem to Work On** asks ALL's team to find a company with one. **Find Sponsors** searches companies near you once ALL approves your chapter.

## Run the lab

**One clear problem, a baseline, eight weeks, and the sponsor's engineer every week.**

| Week | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0 | The kick-off: teams, the mentor, the problem brief and the baseline code |
| 1–3 | Explore the problem, first results. Weekly office hours start |
| 4 | Mid-point check-in: every team gives a 2-minute update, live |
| 5–7 | Build, then practice the demo |
| 8 (Showcase) | Final presentations, judging, results |

1. **Team composition (6–9 members):** one or two experienced ML students per team, the rest learning. Mix years, and schools if you can.
2. **The brief:** the problem in plain English, why the sponsor cares, the data, the baseline, the metric, the constraints.
3. **The mentor** holds weekly office hours, answers async, and never writes the code.
4. **A quiet team gets a nudge.** No commits in a week, and an officer messages the team lead.

**In the dashboard:** **Events → New Event → Innovation Lab** puts the run sheet at the top of Project Work: Sign-up, Teams (**Import Teams** takes a CSV), GitHub (a repo per team), Deliverables (a one-pager or progress update with a due date and reminders), Showcase.

## Showcase and handoff

**The final day is a pitch event. The sponsor gets the code within two weeks.**

1. **Book a room for the day.** Each team gets 5 minutes of demo and 3 of questions. The questions matter most.
2. **Three judges is the right number:** an officer, at least one from the sponsor, and a third seat that flexes (a professor if they'd genuinely add something, otherwise another student or a second sponsor delegate).
3. **Rubric printed and visible:** technical quality 40%, business applicability 40%, presentation 20%.
4. **Prizes split 50/30/20** of the pool and pay out like a hackathon's, never through a chapter account. Announce results the same day.
5. **Hand off within 2 weeks:** cleaned-up code from the top teams, setup notes, and what they trained on.
6. **A letter for every finisher.** Every student who completed the Lab gets one.
7. **30 days after showcase**, email the sponsor about running another.

**In the dashboard:** the **Final** row in Deliverables owns the submission link. Under **Your Chapter → Badges**, **New Certificate** makes one for finishers to add to LinkedIn.
