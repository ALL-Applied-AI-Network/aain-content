# Innovation Labs

A company brings a real problem, mixed teams build for four to eight weeks, and the company leaves with a working prototype. The easiest way into hands-on work.

Innovation Labs are 4- to 8-week industry-sponsored competitions, eight by default, kicked off at the week 5 project kick-off. Sell it to students as a company's name on their project, teammates a few years ahead of them, and a portfolio piece before Christmas break. No experience needed.

## Start here

1. Lead with the problem their team actually has, and settle NDA and IP in the first meeting. Students don't sign NDAs.
2. Four to eight weeks from the week 5 kick-off, teams of six to nine across years, and a sponsor engineer every week.
3. Close the loop: code to the sponsor within two weeks, a letter for every finisher, and an email 30 days later about the next one.

## Pitch a sponsor

**Lead with the problem their team actually has, not the charity. Then settle IP before students see the brief.**

:::steps[Three meetings, then the kick-off]
1. **First meeting (30–45 min).** Learn what they want from students: hires, visibility, a problem solved. Offer pathways, not a price. Have the NDA conversation in meeting 1, every time.
2. **Second meeting (30 min).** Agree the pathway and the budget. $5K is our default prize pool for a Lab.
3. **Third meeting (45 min).** Write the brief together: the data, the metric, and what a win looks like.
4. **Agreement, then kick-off.** Promotion starts four weeks out.
:::

**Students don't sign NDAs.** A required signature lowers sign-ups. Agree up front what data the sponsor shares, who owns what, and whether code can be public afterward. The first Lab lost two weeks in legal back-and-forth by leaving this until September.

**Ask for a mentor.** A sponsor engineer with weekly office hours is what makes it a Lab and not a long hackathon.

**In the dashboard:** **Sponsors → Requests → A Problem to Work On** asks ALL's team to find a company with one. **Sponsors → Companies** shows companies near your school and where your school's alumni work, and checks a list you paste, such as a career-fair list; **Request an Intro from ALL** has ALL's team reach out for you. It opens once your chapter is verified (US chapters only).

## Run the lab

**One clear problem, a baseline, four to eight weeks, and the sponsor's engineer every week.**

![A Lab's timeline on the screen at a kick-off](https://all-ai-network.org/public/playbooks/innovation-labs/kickoff-stage.jpg)

| Week | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0 | The kick-off: teams, the mentor, the problem brief and the baseline code |
| 1–3 | Explore the problem, first results. Weekly office hours start |
| 4 | Mid-point check-in: every team gives a 2-minute update, live |
| 5–7 | Build, then practice the demo |
| 8 (Showcase) | Final presentations, judging, results |

Eight weeks is the default, and New Event also offers six or four. A shorter Lab keeps the same beats, closer together.

:::cards[What makes it work]
- **Team composition (6–9 members).** One or two experienced ML students per team, the rest learning. Mix years, and schools if you can.
- **The brief.** The problem in plain English, why the sponsor cares, the data, the baseline, the metric, the constraints.
- **The mentor.** Holds weekly office hours, answers async, and never writes the code.
- **A quiet team gets a nudge.** No commits in a week, and an officer messages the team lead.
:::

**In the dashboard:**

1. **Events → New Event → Innovation Lab** asks a few questions: when, where, whose problem, teams, sign-up, stages and submissions.
2. On the event, **Stages** gives the kick-off, the mid-point and the showcase each a date and their own QR.
3. **Project Work** opens with the run sheet: Sign-Up, Teams (**Import Teams** takes a CSV), GitHub (a repo per team), Deliverables (a one-pager or progress update with a due date and reminders) and Finished projects.
4. If the event has a survey, its answers sit in the event's **Surveys** block, with a button that emails everyone who hasn't replied.

## Showcase and handoff

**The final day is a pitch event. The sponsor gets the code within two weeks.**

![A winning team from the first Lab with its prize](https://all-ai-network.org/public/playbooks/innovation-labs/showcase-winners.jpg)

:::numbers
- **5 + 3 min.** Each team demos for 5 minutes, then takes 3 of questions. The questions matter most. Book a room for the day.
- **3 judges.** Three judges is the right number: an officer, at least one from the sponsor, and a seat that flexes (a professor if they'd genuinely add something).
- **Same day.** Announce the results before anyone leaves.
:::

:::split[Rubric printed and visible]
- **40%** Technical quality
- **40%** Business applicability
- **20%** Presentation
:::

:::split[Prizes, split of the pool]
- **50%** First place
- **30%** Second place
- **20%** Third place
:::

**Prizes never pass through a chapter account.** They pay out like a hackathon's.

:::steps[After the showcase]
1. **Code within 2 weeks.** Cleaned-up code from the top teams, setup notes, and what they trained on.
2. **A letter for every finisher.** Every student who completed the Lab gets one.
3. **30 days after showcase.** Email the sponsor about running another.
:::

**In the dashboard:** the **Final** row in Deliverables owns the submission link. Under **Members → Badges**, **New Certificate** makes one for finishers to add to LinkedIn.
