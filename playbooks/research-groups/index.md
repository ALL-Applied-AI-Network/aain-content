# Research Groups

A small team of advanced students, one mentor and one question, for about six months, ending in a paper, a poster or a release.

Research groups are 6-month projects for teams of 3–8, running alongside everything else from the week 5 kick-off into spring. They're for students who want to publish; newer students are better served by an Innovation Lab. The founding chapter's research groups have published 50+ papers.

## Start here

1. Mentors first, topics second, students third: lock mentors and topics over the summer and announce them in week 1.
2. Set the bar in the sign-up form and hold it: 3–5 hours/week, weekly group meetings, at least one deliverable every 4 weeks.
3. Pick the publication target by end of Month 2, and write the introduction while experiments still run.

## Form the group

**A group is only as good as its commitment. Set the bar high and say it before anyone signs up.**

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Summer | Recruit mentors. Each proposes a topic they genuinely care about |
| Week 1 | Announce topics and mentors at the "What is [your club]?" event |
| Week 2 | Open sign-ups: students rank their top 3 topics and state their weekly hours |
| Week 5 | The kick-off: teams and mentors revealed, and each group meets that day |

1. **The mentor** (faculty, a grad student, or an industry researcher) sets direction and meets the group weekly.
2. **The project lead**, a student, runs the meetings and owns the timeline.
3. **The bar:** 3–5 hours/week, weekly group meetings, at least one deliverable every 4 weeks. Two missed meetings without notice and a member is out.

**In the dashboard:** **Events → New Event → Research Group** sets up one group: its problem (Our Own Question, A Partner's Problem, or Find Us a Problem, which files a request with ALL), one team of 3 to 8, and check-ins every 2 or 4 weeks. A year's groups sit together under one cohort event.

## Run experiments

**Read, design, test, write. A deliverable every four weeks keeps a group from drifting.**

| Month | Deliverable |
|---|---|
| 1 | Literature review summary |
| 2 | Experiment plan + first baseline results, and the publication target |
| 3 | Main experiments running. Draft the introduction, related work and method |
| 4 | Results, analysis and a first paper draft |
| 5 | Final paper and presentation practice |
| 6 | Submit, and present |

1. **Between milestones, a 2-week informal check-in.** Fifteen minutes of "what are you working on?" catches drift before the next deadline does.
2. **Meetings alternate** between a paper (one member summarizes, the mentor fills gaps) and experiment work (the project lead runs the agenda).
3. **Log every experiment** (config, seed, data, metric, result) and commit to the group's shared repo every week.

**In the dashboard:** the check-ins are the group's deliverables, with reminders before each is due. **Compute → Find Compute** is where a group asks for GPUs: campus hardware, research programs or cloud credits, usually free.

## Publish

**A research group that doesn't publish is a study group. Pick the target early and write as you go.**

| Target | What it takes |
|---|---|
| Technical blog post | You publish it |
| Open-source release | You ship it, with a README |
| arXiv preprint | No review, and the work becomes citable |
| A campus research competition | An internal review |
| MICS (the regional conference) | Peer review. The default target |
| Workshop paper | Peer review, selective |
| Main conference paper | Peer review, very selective |

1. **Authorship:** everyone who contributed significantly. The biggest contributor goes first and the mentor last.
2. **Rejected?** Read the reviews, post to arXiv, and send it to the next venue.
3. **Recognition** such as graduation cords goes through your registrar. Ask early, and don't promise it at sign-up.

**In the dashboard:** the group's final hand-in is its paper or demo, the last row in its deliverables.
